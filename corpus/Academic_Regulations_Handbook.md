### PREAMBLE AND STATUTORY ENACTMENT

Pursuant to the powers vested in the Office of the University Registrar under Section 14 of the University Charter of 1968, and in accordance with the statutory resolutions ratified by the Academic Senate, this *Academic Regulations Handbook* (hereinafter referred to as the "Codex") constitutes the supreme and definitive governing instrument regarding matriculation, instructional progression, evaluation metrics, scholastic standing, examinations, and disciplinary remedies across all constituent faculties, schools, and academic divisions of the University.

Ignorance of these statutory regulations shall not be admitted as a defense, mitigating circumstance, or procedural plea in any administrative, academic, or appellate proceeding. Every student, upon signature of the matriculation roll and receipt of institutional credentials, agrees unequivocally to be bound by the directives, strictures, penalties, and remedial courses of action delineated within this Codex.

## CHAPTER 1: GENERAL MATRICULATION AND CREDIT REQUIREMENTS

### Section 1.1: Institutional Definitions and Terminology
* **Section 1.1.1:** An **"Academic Credit Unit" (ACU)** represents a standardized quantified measure of instructional delivery and student workload. Precisely, one (1) lecture credit represents one hour (60 minutes) of didactic instruction per week over a canonical fifteen-week semester; one (1) laboratory or practical credit denotes precisely two hours (120 minutes) of supervised empirical, studio, or laboratory coursework per week over the identical semester duration.
* **Section 1.1.2:** **"Matriculated Status"** denotes the legal and institutional condition of a candidate who has satisfied all entry prerequisites, verified prior credentials with the Registrar’s Directorate of Admissions, remitted all statutory fiscal dues, and executed the Matriculation Declaration.
* **Section 1.1.3:** **"Normal Academic Load"** refers to the prescribed baseline of credits assigned to an undergraduate or postgraduate cohort within any standard semester as gazetted in the relevant Faculty Program Syllabi.

### Section 1.2: Degree Conferral Minimum Credit Architecture
* **Section 1.2.1:** **Undergraduate Baccalaureate Degrees:**
  * Candidates pursuing a standard four-year Bachelor of Science (B.Sc.), Bachelor of Arts (B.A.), or Bachelor of Business Administration (B.B.A.) degree must satisfactorily complete and accumulate not fewer than one hundred and twenty-eight (128) cumulative credit units.
  * Candidates enrolled in the Bachelor of Engineering (B.Eng.), Bachelor of Technology (B.Tech.), or allied five-year professional curricula must accumulate not fewer than one hundred and sixty (160) cumulative credit units.
* **Section 1.2.2:** **Postgraduate Master’s Degrees:**
  * Master of Science (M.Sc.), Master of Arts (M.A.), and Master of Technology (M.Tech.) programs require the successful completion of no fewer than thirty-six (36) graduate credit units, encompassing both structured advanced coursework and an empirical research dissertation defended before an external viva voce tribunal.
* **Section 1.2.3:** **Distribution of Curricular Load:**
  * Every degree program shall maintain a strict structural balance:
    * Core Foundational / Major Discipline: Minimum 60% of total aggregate credits.
    * Cognate and Multidisciplinary Electives: Minimum 20% of total aggregate credits.
    * Institutional General Education Breadth Requirements: Exactly 15% of total aggregate credits.
    * Capstone Thesis / Final Research Project: Exactly 5% of total aggregate credits.

### Section 1.3: Semester Credit Thresholds and Registration Limits
* **Section 1.3.1:** **Minimum and Maximum Enrollment:**
  * No full-time undergraduate student shall be permitted to register for fewer than twelve (12) credits in any given regular semester, save for candidates in their terminal semester who require fewer credits to satisfy remaining degree conferral requirements upon explicit written dispensation from the Registrar.
  * The maximum permissible semester load for an undergraduate scholar in good academic standing is twenty-one (21) credits.
* **Section 1.3.2:** **Academic Credit Overload Petitions:**
  * An enrollment exceeding twenty-one (21) credits, up to an absolute ceiling of twenty-four (24) credits, may be granted solely to students who maintain a Cumulative Grade Point Average (CGPA) of not less than 3.750 on a 4.000 scale. Such an application must be countersigned by the Head of Department, endorsed by the Academic Dean, and lodged with the Registrar’s Academic Records Bureau no later than ten (10) university business days prior to the commencement of instructional delivery.
* **Section 1.3.3:** **Audit and Non-Credit Courses:**
  * A student may register for a maximum of one (1) non-credit audit course per term. Audited coursework does not contribute to GPA computations, nor does it satisfy prerequisite thresholds, but it shall appear on the official institutional transcript with the designation "AU" provided that the candidate satisfies the mandatory minimum attendance strictures set forth in Section 3.1.2.

### Section 1.4: Course Add/Drop and Term Withdrawal Protocols
* **Section 1.4.1:** The official Add/Drop Period shall terminate precisely at 17:00 hours on the tenth (10th) working day following the official opening of the instructional semester. Course changes requested after this deadline are void *ab initio*.
* **Section 1.4.2:** Voluntary course withdrawal executed between the eleventh (11th) instructional day and the end of the eighth (8th) instructional week shall result in the permanent transcription of the designation "W" (Withdrawn). The "W" designation bears no grade point value and does not prejudice the GPA, but the full tuition fee liability remains accrued.
* **Section 1.4.3:** Any course abandonments executed following the conclusion of the eighth (8th) instructional week shall automatically be classified as an unauthorized withdrawal and recorded irrevocably as a failing grade of "F" (Zero Grade Points).

## CHAPTER 2: THE UNIFORM GRADING SYSTEM (GRADES A TO F)

### Section 2.1: The Grading Scale, Points Allocation, and Qualitative Descriptors
* **Section 2.1.1:** Evaluation across all constituent faculties must conform strictly to the standard Four-Point (4.000) Letter Grading Paradigm. No faculty member or academic unit possesses the discretionary authority to deviate from the numeric conversions and qualitative descriptors ratified hereunder:

* **Section 2.2.3:** Both SGPA and CGPA shall be computed and permanently rendered to precisely three decimal places, rounded mathematically at the fourth decimal place.

### Section 2.3: Non-Numeric and Administrative Transcript Symbols
* **Section 2.3.1:** **"I" — Incomplete:**
  * Assigned exclusively where a candidate, through severe, incontrovertible, documented medical trauma or extreme personal bereavement, was rendered incapable of completing terminal coursework or final examinations. The "I" grade is provisional and shall lapse automatically into an absolute "F" precisely forty-five (45) calendar days after the close of the examination period if remaining assessments are not adjudicated.
* **Section 2.3.2:** **"IP" — In Progress:**
  * Reserved strictly for multi-semester capstone investigations, graduate dissertations, or formal clinical internships whose academic design spans multiple terms.
* **Section 2.3.3:** **"CR / NC" — Credit / No Credit:**
  * Applicable solely to designated experiential practicums, non-credit zero-hour safety modules, or special physical education courses. "CR" requires attainment of at least 60.00% (Grade C equivalent); anything lower receives "NC".

### Section 2.4: Transcript Authentication and Grade Freezing
* **Section 2.4.1:** Once grades have been entered into the Central Student Information System (CSIS) and formally approved by the Board of Examiners, they are deemed immutable. No grade alteration may occur unless an overt ministerial or clerical tabulation error is proven via formal affidavit sworn by the course instructor and endorsed in writing by both the Head of Department and the Registrar.

## CHAPTER 3: ATTENDANCE POLICIES AND INSTRUCTIONAL AUDITS

### Section 3.1: Mandatory Instructional Attendance Thresholds
* **Section 3.1.1:** Regular and punctual classroom engagement constitutes an irrevocable academic obligation. Higher education requires didactic interaction, collaborative interrogation, and sustained empirical discourse.
* **Section 3.1.2:** A student must maintain a minimum of 75% physical attendance to be eligible for end-semester examinations.
* **Section 3.1.3:** The calculation of attendance shall be purely programmatic and objective:
  $$	ext{Attendance Percentage} = \left( rac{	ext{Number of Instructional Hours Attended}}{	ext{Total Instructional Hours Timetabled and Delivered}} ight) 	imes 100$$
* **Section 3.1.4:** Any student whose aggregate attendance falls below the mandatory threshold stipulated in Section 3.1.2 without having secured a formal statutory exemption as provided under Section 8.4.1 shall be systematically classified as **"Attendance Debarred" (Grade "AD")** for the course in question.
* **Section 3.1.5:** An "AD" designation carries a grade point metric of 0.000, incurs full tuition liability, and categorically denies the candidate physical entry to the final examination hall.

### Section 3.2: Recordation Mechanics, Biometrics, and Verification
* **Section 3.2.1:** Every instructor of record is legally bound to record attendance at every scheduled lecture, recitation, laboratory session, and field tutorial through the University Centralized Biometric Logging System or verified digital signature roll within thirty (30) minutes of the session commencement.
* **Section 3.2.2:** Fraudulent manipulation of attendance logs, including biometric proxy check-ins, barcode forgery, or the submission of falsified physical roll signatures, constitutes Gross Institutional Misconduct under Section 6.2 and results in immediate suspension for not less than one full academic year.

### Section 3.3: Mid-Semester Attendance Warnings and Deficiencies
* **Section 3.3.1:** At the conclusion of the fourth (4th) and eighth (8th) weeks of instruction, the Registrar’s Academic Records Bureau shall issue automated Formal Attendance Deficiency Notices to all matriculants exhibiting attendance levels below 80%.
* **Section 3.3.2:** Receipt of an Attendance Deficiency Notice obligates the student to confer immediately with their assigned Faculty Academic Advisor to formulate an Academic Correction Plan. Failure to execute such a conference within five (5) working days shall be cited in any subsequent debarment appeal as prima facie evidence of bad faith.

## CHAPTER 4: REGULATION AND ADMINISTRATION OF EXAMINATIONS

### Section 4.1: Examination Schedules, Seating Protocols, and Identification
* **Section 4.1.1:** The Office of the University Registrar publishes the authoritative Master Examination Schedule not less than twenty-one (21) calendar days prior to the commencement of the assessment window. No departmental authority or faculty board may reschedule an examination without the explicit written warrant of the Registrar.
* **Section 4.1.2:** Candidates must present themselves outside the assigned examination hall precisely thirty (30) minutes prior to the scheduled start time. Entry shall be conditioned upon the presentation of a valid University Biometric Identification Card and the official Examination Hall Admittance Ticket.
* **Section 4.1.3:** Candidates arriving more than thirty (30) minutes past the gazetted commencement time shall be strictly barred from entering the examination venue, receiving an unexcused absence and an automatic grade of "F".

### Section 4.2: Hall Conduct, Prohibited Items, and Security
* **Section 4.2.1:** Candidates are strictly forbidden from bringing into any examination venue any electronic storage devices, cellular phones, programmable microprocessors, smart watches, communicative apparatus, unverified loose papers, notebooks, or unauthorized formula compilations.
* **Section 4.2.2:** Possession of unauthorized materials within an examination hall, irrespective of whether an attempt was made to actively consult them, constitutes an irrebuttable presumption of cheating under the Academic Dishonesty Statutes.
* **Section 4.2.3:** Candidates must maintain absolute silence throughout the assessment. Any unauthorized communication, gesture, exchange of stationery, or glance toward another candidate’s examination materials shall be recorded as an immediate breach of examination integrity.

### Section 4.3: Invigilation Protocol, Incident Logging, and Confiscation
* **Section 4.3.1:** The Chief Invigilator exercises full statutory authority over the conduct of the examination venue. In any instance of observed academic dishonesty:
  * The invigilator shall immediately confiscate all relevant illicit instruments, notes, or electronic apparatus.
  * The candidate's primary answer script shall be endorsed across its cover sheet with the annotation: *"BREACH OBSERVED AT [TIME] BY [INVIGILATOR NAME]"* and impounded.
  * The candidate shall be issued a secondary answer script and instructed to resume writing, to ensure that if an appellate tribunal subsequently finds the candidate innocent, no instructional writing time has been irrecoverably lost.
  * The candidate must remain in the hall until the formal expiration of the assessment period, whereupon the Chief Invigilator shall execute a standardized Incident Dossier and serve a copy directly to the Registrar's Disciplinary Liaison Officer.

### Section 4.4: Deferred Examinations and Emergency Absences
* **Section 4.4.1:** A candidate who is prevented from presenting themselves for an examination by reason of sudden acute illness, sudden inpatient hospitalization, or immediate bereavement of a first-degree relative must formally notify the Registrar within forty-eight (48) hours of the scheduled assessment.
* **Section 4.4.2:** Retroactive petitions for deferred examinations filed after the publication of grades or without verified institutional clinical confirmation shall be denied summarily.

## CHAPTER 5: ACADEMIC PROBATION, REMEDIATION, AND DISMISSAL

### Section 5.1: Supplementary Examinations and Course Remediation
* **Section 5.1:** Students receiving a grade of D or F may sit for the supplementary improvement examination during the summer semester.
* **Section 5.1.1:** The supplementary improvement examination shall be comprehensive in nature, assessing the totality of the programmatic curriculum for the course.
* **Section 5.1.2:** Grade Capping Metric: Following successful completion of a supplementary improvement examination, the newly recorded grade shall be capped at a maximum of "B" (3.000 Grade Points). The historical record of the original deficient grade shall remain marked on the internal institutional transcript with the superscription "IMP" indicating supplementary remediation.
* **Section 5.1.3:** Candidates may not attempt more than two (2) supplementary improvement examinations within a single summer instructional term without special dispensation from the Academic Senate.

### Section 5.2: Thresholds of Academic Standing
* **Section 5.2.1:** **Good Academic Standing:** A student is defined as being in Good Academic Standing whenever their CGPA equals or exceeds 2.000 on a 4.000 scale.
* **Section 5.2.2:** **Academic Warning:** A student whose single-term SGPA falls below 2.000, while maintaining a CGPA of 2.000 or greater, shall be issued a formal letter of Academic Warning. This status restricts total subsequent registration to a maximum of sixteen (16) credits.

### Section 5.3: Formal Academic Probation Mechanisms
* **Section 5.3.1:** **Placement on Academic Probation:** A student shall be placed on formal Academic Probation if their Cumulative Grade Point Average (CGPA) falls below 2.000 at the end of any regular instructional semester.
* **Section 5.3.2:** **Probationary Strictures:**
  * A student on Academic Probation is legally barred from enrolling in more than thirteen (13) credit units per term.
  * A student on Academic Probation is disqualified from holding any elected or appointed leadership office within student governance, varsity athletics, or campus organizations.
  * The student must report bi-weekly to the University Academic Success Center for formal counseling and remedial monitoring.
* **Section 5.3.3:** **Restoration of Good Standing:** A probationary student who raises their CGPA to 2.000 or above by the conclusion of the following regular semester shall be fully restored to Good Academic Standing.

### Section 5.4: Extended Probation and Academic Dismissal
* **Section 5.4.1:** **Extended Probation:** If, after one regular semester on Academic Probation, the student fails to achieve a CGPA of 2.000, but demonstrates clear upward scholastic trajectory by attaining an SGPA of not less than 2.300 in that term, the student may be granted a single, non-renewable semester of Extended Academic Probation.
* **Section 5.4.2:** **Academic Dismissal (Disqualification):** A student shall be permanently academically dismissed from the University under the following conditions:
  * Failure to achieve a CGPA of at least 2.000 following two consecutive semesters of Academic Probation;
  * Accumulation of an SGPA below 1.000 in two consecutive regular semesters, irrespective of cumulative standing;
  * Failure to satisfy all graduation credit requirements within the Maximum Permissible Timeframe, defined as 150% of the nominal program duration (e.g., six academic years for a four-year baccalaureate degree).
* **Section 5.4.3:** An academically dismissed student is permanently stripped of matriculated status and is barred from re-enrolling in any degree-granting program within the University for a minimum period of three (3) calendar years.

## CHAPTER 6: DISCIPLINARY APPEALS AND ADJUDICATIVE DUE PROCESS

### Section 6.1: Jurisdiction and Composition of the Academic Appeals Tribunal
* **Section 6.1.1:** The Senate Academic Appeals Tribunal (SAAT) constitutes the supreme quasi-judicial appellate authority of the University regarding academic malpractice, attendance debarment, grade contested arbitrations, and disciplinary expulsions.
* **Section 6.1.2:** The Tribunal shall be constituted by appointment of the Academic Senate and shall comprise:
  * A Senior Faculty Dean (Chairperson, possessing voting privileges only in the event of an equal division);
  * Three tenured Senior Professors representing distinct non-involved faculties;
  * The University Legal Counsel (sitting *ex-officio* as legal advisor without vote);
  * One elected representative of the Student Bar or Student Government in good academic standing (non-voting observer);
  * The Registrar or a designated Deputy Registrar serving as Recording Secretary.

### Section 6.2: Categories of Academic Malpractice and Associated Sanctions
* **Section 6.2.1:** **Plagiarism and Unattributed Attribution:**
  * Minor Infraction (less than 15% unattributed textual reproduction on minor coursework): Zero grade on the assignment, mandatory enrollment in the Institutional Research Ethics Clinic.
  * Major Infraction (greater than 15% direct copying, or any submission of commercially acquired, AI-generated, or ghost-written materials): Irrevocable failure ("F") in the course, alongside a mandatory one-semester disciplinary suspension.
* **Section 6.2.2:** **Examination Malpractice:**
  * Possession of unauthorized materials or communicative apparatus: Grade of "F" in the course, plus a minimum one-year (two-semester) suspension.
  * Impersonation (procuring another individual to sit an examination or sitting an examination for another): Immediate, permanent, and irrevocable Expulsion from the University, with notification transmitted to the national registry of universities.
  * Theft or unauthorized distribution of examination papers prior to the official administration time: Immediate expulsion and civil criminal referral to law enforcement authorities.

### Section 6.3: Appellate Procedural Roadmap
* **Section 6.3.1:** **Filing Deadline:** Any petition appealing an academic disciplinary decision or grading sanction must be lodged with the Office of the University Registrar within precisely ten (10) university business days from the formal electronic transmission of the initial judgment. Late filings are barred by laches and shall be dismissed without review.
* **Section 6.3.2:** **Pleading Requirements:** Every appeal petition must contain:
  * A detailed factual narrative stating the procedural irregularity, bias, or emerging new empirical evidence upon which the appeal rests;
  * Affidavits or documentary evidence substantiating each factual averment;
  * The statutory relief sought.
* **Section 6.3.3:** **Pre-Hearing Admissibility Review:** Within five (5) working days of receipt, the Chair of the SAAT and the University Legal Counsel shall conduct a preliminary documentary review to determine if a genuine prima facie claim has been stated. Frivolous, repetitive, or unsubstantiated appeals shall be dismissed summarily at this juncture.

### Section 6.4: Due Process Rights at Formal Hearing
* **Section 6.4.1:** The appellant possesses the inviolable right to:
  * Receive written notice of the specific allegations, evidentiary dossier, and witness lists not less than seven (7) calendar days prior to the hearing date;
  * Present documentary exhibits, tender sworn witness testimony, and submit an oral defense before the assembled Tribunal;
  * Be accompanied by an academic advisor or legal counsel from the University community acting as a non-pleading silent advisor (legal representatives may not address the Tribunal directly save upon explicit leave of the Chair);
  * Cross-examine any institutional witnesses testifying in support of the disciplinary charges.
* **Section 6.4.2:** **Standard of Proof:** The Tribunal shall determine liability based upon the evidentiary standard of the **"Preponderance of Clear and Convincing Evidence"** (a threshold exceeding simple balance of probabilities, reflecting the grave institutional consequences of disciplinary determinations).

### Section 6.5: Finality of Verdicts and Presidential Discretion
* **Section 6.5.1:** The SAAT shall render its written reasoned judgment within fourteen (14) calendar days of the formal close of hearings.
* **Section 6.5.2:** The verdict of the SAAT is conclusive and binding upon all operational organs of the University. The only subsequent remedy lies in a formal Petition for Executive Clemency addressed directly to the University President/Vice-Chancellor within seven (7) calendar days of the Tribunal’s decree. The President may grant clemency solely on grounds of egregious procedural breach or newly discovered exonerating evidence of a definitive nature.

## CHAPTER 7: SPECIAL LEAVES OF ABSENCE, WITHDRAWALS, AND CANCELLATIONS

### Section 7.1: Voluntary Leave of Absence (VLOA)
* **Section 7.1.1:** A matriculated student in good academic standing may petition for a Voluntary Leave of Absence for a maximum cumulative duration of two (2) academic semesters throughout their degree program.
* **Section 7.1.2:** Applications for VLOA must be submitted no later than the close of the Add/Drop period of the semester for which the leave is desired. Retroactive leaves of absence are prohibited under all circumstances.
* **Section 7.1.3:** During an approved VLOA, the candidate's active student status is suspended: they may not attend classes, utilize laboratory or computational infrastructure, reside in university housing, or sit for examinations.

### Section 7.2: Involuntary Medical Leave of Absence (IMLOA)
* **Section 7.2.1:** The University Registrar, upon formal recommendation of the University Chief Medical Officer and the Director of Student Health Services, may place a student on an Involuntary Medical Leave of Absence when it is empirically determined that the student’s physical or psychiatric condition presents an immediate danger to their own safety or the safety of the campus community, or renders the student incapable of participating in academic life even with reasonable accommodations.
* **Section 7.2.2:** Reinstatement following an IMLOA requires comprehensive medical clearance by an independent medical board empaneled by the University at the student's expense.

## CHAPTER 8: ATTENDANCE EXEMPTIONS, STATUTORY CONDONATION, AND WAIVERS

### Section 8.1: Nature of Exemptions
* **Section 8.1.1:** Attendance at all scheduled instructional sessions is fundamentally obligatory as enacted under Section 3.1.2. The mechanisms outlined within this Chapter represent extraordinary administrative remedies designed to accommodate verifiable catastrophes, systemic medical impediments, and authorized representations of the University at recognized academic or athletic symposia.
* **Section 8.1.2:** Under no circumstances shall an exemption be granted purely on the grounds of employment conflicts, personal vacations, extracurricular domestic travel, or general administrative inconvenience.

### Section 8.2: Institutional Representation Absences
* **Section 8.2.1:** Students selected to represent the University in national or international intercollegiate academic competitions, scientific conferences, or recognized athletic tournaments may claim authorized absence credits for the precise days of event participation and essential travel.
* **Section 8.2.2:** To qualify for institutional representation dispensations, the formal roster must be certified by the appropriate Faculty Dean or University Athletics Director and lodged with the Registrar at least fourteen (14) days prior to the event. The maximum absence allowable under this category shall not exceed 10% of total course contact hours.

### Section 8.3: Bereavement and Urgent Compassionate Leaves
* **Section 8.3.1:** A compassionate absence of up to a maximum of five (5) consecutive instructional working days may be granted upon the death of a member of the student’s immediate family (defined strictly as a parent, legal guardian, sibling, spouse, or child).
* **Section 8.3.2:** Official certified death certificates, obituaries, and evidence of kinship must be submitted to the Registrar’s Office within ten (10) working days of the student’s resumption of classes.

### Section 8.4: Medical Exemptions, Chronic Infirmity, and Statutory Condonation
* **Section 8.4.1 (Exemptions):** Students with sanctioned medical leave must have at least 65% aggregate attendance; however, the Academic Dean may condone attendance down to 60% upon formal petition.
* **Section 8.4.2:** **Evidentiary Threshold for Sanctioned Medical Leave:**
  * For medical leave to be legally "sanctioned" under Section 8.4.1, the student must submit an official medical dossier issued by a fully registered medical practitioner or the University Health Clinic.
  * The medical certificate must clearly detail the nature of the incapacitation, the exact dates of clinical hospitalization or mandatory bed rest, and must be endorsed by the University Medical Officer within seven (7) calendar days of the student's discharge or recovery.
* **Section 8.4.3:** **Procedural Rigor for Formal Condonation Petitions (Down to 60%):**
  * In the event that a student's attendance falls below 65% but remains at or above the absolute statutory floor of 60%, the student may initiate a formal Condonation Petition directed to the Academic Dean.
  * The petition must include:
    1. A formal Letter of Appeal setting out the exceptional medical circumstances;
    2. The fully validated Medical Sanction Certificate ratified under Section 8.4.2;
    3. A comprehensive academic evaluation from the course instructor certifying that the student has completed all intermediate assignments, continuous assessments, and laboratory requirements to a satisfactory standard.
  * The Academic Dean retains sole and unfettered discretion to grant or deny condonation. Condonation below 60% is an absolute legal impossibility under this Codex; no officer, committee, or board of the University possesses the legal power to condone attendance beneath the 60% aggregate threshold.

## CHAPTER 9: DEGREE AUDIT, COMMENCEMENT, AND HONORS CLASSIFICATIONS

### Section 9.1: Degree Audit and Clearance
* **Section 9.1.1:** Prior to candidacy confirmation for degree conferral, the Registrar’s Directorate of Academic Records shall conduct a thorough Degree Audit of every candidate’s academic record.
* **Section 9.1.2:** A candidate is clear for graduation only when:
  * All minimum credit requirements detailed in Chapter 1 have been fully accumulated;
  * The candidate has attained a final Cumulative Grade Point Average (CGPA) of not less than 2.000;
  * No pending disciplinary sanctions, unresolved academic dishonesty charges, or incomplete ("I") grades remain on the transcript;
  * All financial liabilities, library charges, laboratory apparatus liabilities, and residence fees have been settled and certified by the Comptroller.

### Section 9.2: Baccalaureate Honors Classifications
* **Section 9.2.1:** Undergraduate baccalaureate degrees shall be awarded with Latin or Programmatic Honors based strictly on final CGPA metrics achieved at the conclusion of all degree requirements:
  * **Summa Cum Laude (First Class with Distinction):** CGPA of 3.850 to 4.000, provided the candidate has incurred zero disciplinary sanctions, zero academic probations, and completed the degree within nominal timeframe.
  * **Magna Cum Laude (First Class):** CGPA of 3.650 to 3.849.
  * **Cum Laude (Second Class Upper Division):** CGPA of 3.300 to 3.649.
  * **Pass (Second Class Lower Division):** CGPA of 2.500 to 3.299.
  * **Third Class / Ordinary Pass:** CGPA of 2.000 to 2.499.
* **Section 9.2.2:** A candidate who has repeated a course due to failure or sat for a supplementary improvement examination under Section 5.1 shall be permanently ineligible for the award of *Summa Cum Laude*.
